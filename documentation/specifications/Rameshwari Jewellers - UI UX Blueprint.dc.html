Rameshwari Jewellers · Gold • Silver • Bridal Kundan · Jaipur

UI/UX Blueprint

A luxury black-and-gold catalogue experience, designed mobile-first and built to survive every theme, builder and redesign the business will go through.
Standing on the approved architecture

Nothing in this document changes the business architecture, the post type names, the database structure, the taxonomy or the customer model. Those are settled. This is the layer above them: what a customer sees, in what order, and what happens when they touch it. Where a screen needs data, it names the approved field — it never invents one.

The four design principles

PrincipleWhat it rules out
Restraint is the luxuryNo gradient floods, no gold-filled buttons, no drop shadows on everything. The jewellery is the only ornament on the page. Gold appears as a line, a hairline border and a small mark.
Black is a stage, not a moodThe near-black ground exists so gold photography glows against it. Nothing else may be bright enough to compete with a product image.
Every screen ends in WhatsAppNo screen is a dead end. If a customer can see a piece, a reel or a showroom, they can start a conversation about it in one tap.
Mobile is the real siteDesktop is the derivative. Every layout is designed at 390 px first and widened, never the reverse.

1 · Homepage

1.1 Section order and why

#SectionJobRationale
1HeroEstablish the brand in one glanceOne piece, one line, one action. A customer decides in under two seconds whether this is a serious jeweller.
2Featured categoriesRoute the visitorCustomers arrive knowing the kind of thing they want — नथ, झुमका, मंगलसूत्र — not a specific piece. Routing beats browsing.

9ShowroomsConvert to a visitThe real conversion for a local jeweller. Both branches, both with directions.
10FooterCatch everything elseNavigation, contact, social, trust marks.

Ten sections and no more. Every additional homepage section costs scroll depth on the sections that convert, so anything not on this list belongs on its own page.

2.3 Sticky behaviour

At topTop bar visible, main header transparent over the hero, no border. Full 84 px.

The condensed header must never change height while the menu is open or while a drawer is showing — a resizing header under an open panel is the most common cause of a menu that jumps under the cursor.

2.6 The cart icon — resolved as an Enquiry List

The header calls for a cart icon, and this is a catalogue with no checkout. Rather than show a cart that cannot check out, the icon is designed as an enquiry list: a customer adds several pieces and sends them as one WhatsApp message instead of five.

AspectDesign
IconA bag outline, identical in weight to search and wishlist. Badge shows the item count.
PanelRight-hand drawer. Rows of thumbnail, name, code, weight, remove.
Primary action"Send enquiry on WhatsApp" — one message listing every code and name, built from the approved WhatsApp template engine.
Secondary"Clear list" as a text link.
No priceNo subtotal, no quantity stepper, no checkout. Nothing that implies a transaction.
Empty state"Add pieces you want to ask about" plus a link to New Arrivals.

Owner decision: if the enquiry list is not wanted, the icon is removed from the header entirely rather than shown as a non-functional cart. A cart that does nothing is worse than no cart.

3.2 Interaction behaviour

EventResponse
Hover "Categories"Panel opens after a 120 ms intent delay, fading and rising 8 px over 200 ms. The delay prevents the panel firing as the cursor crosses on its way elsewhere.
Hover a rootIts children replace column 2 instantly. Columns 3 and 4 clear rather than showing stale children.
Diagonal movementA cursor travelling diagonally toward the next column keeps the current row active for 300 ms. Without this, a hover menu loses the item the moment the pointer clips a neighbour.
Click a rowNavigates to that category. Every row is a real link at every level — a parent is never a dead label.
Leave the panelCloses after 200 ms. Immediate close makes the menu feel twitchy.
KeyboardEnter or Space opens. Arrow keys move within a column, Right enters the child column, Left returns, Escape closes and restores focus to the trigger. Focus is trapped inside the open panel.
Touch on a hybrid deviceFirst tap opens, second tap navigates.
Empty categoryA category with no visible products and no children does not appear at all.

3.3 Mobile mega menu

AspectDesign
PatternFull-screen drill-down, one level per screen. Not an accordion — four nested accordions on a phone produce a 40-row list with no sense of place.
Screen headerBack chevron with the parent's name, the current level's name as the title, and a close X.
Rows56 px tall. Hindi name on the first line, English beneath in 12 px muted. A chevron marks rows with children; rows without one navigate.
Parent accessThe first row of every child screen is "View all in {parent}" — so a parent category is always reachable, never only a gateway.
TransitionHorizontal slide, 240 ms, forward left and back right, so depth is felt.
Back buttonThe device back gesture moves up one level before closing the menu.
FeaturedShown as a single image card at the top of the root screen only.
SearchA search field is pinned above the list — at 1,000 categories, search is faster than any tree.

3.4 Category images in the menu

PlacementRule
Root columnNo images. Text only — five images in a narrow column is noise.
Levels 2–4Optional 32 px circular thumbnail from the category image, left of the label. If a category has no image, the row shows no placeholder and the text aligns left with its siblings that do — mixed alignment is less ugly than a grey circle.

4.3 Slider rules

The hero defaults to a single static slide. A slider is available but governed, because a carousel on a slow connection downloads three images to show one, and most visitors never see slide two.

RuleDetail
Maximum slidesThree. The control does not accept a fourth.
AutoplayOff by default. If enabled, 7 seconds per slide, and it stops permanently on any user interaction.
Pause controlAn autoplaying slider must show a visible pause button. This is an accessibility requirement, not a preference.
LoadingOnly slide one is eager. Slides two and three load after the first paint.
IndicatorsThin gold dashes, not dots. Current dash is solid; the others are 30% opacity.
TransitionCross-fade, 400 ms. No slide-in, no zoom, no parallax.
MobileSwipe enabled; autoplay always off regardless of the setting.
Reduced motionAutoplay disabled entirely; the slider becomes manual.
RecommendationUse one slide. Use three only for a genuine festival or bridal campaign running alongside the evergreen hero.

5.2 Category card anatomy

PartSpecification
ShapePortrait 4:5 image with an 8 px radius. Circles are avoided — jewellery silhouettes are long and a circle crops them badly.
ImageThe category image from the approved term field, shot on black. Where one is missing the card shows a gold monogram mark on a slightly lifted surface — a designed empty state, never a grey box.
LabelHindi name, 16 px, off-white, directly beneath the image. English reading, 12 px, muted, on the second line. Both always shown — customers use both.
CountOptional, 11 px muted, "24 pieces". Hidden when the count is under 3 so a thin category does not advertise its thinness.
TargetThe whole card. Minimum 44 px in both dimensions at every breakpoint.
AlignmentCentre-aligned text under a centred image. This is the one place the design centres; everywhere else is flush left.

5.3 States

StateTreatment
DefaultImage at full opacity, no border, no shadow.
HoverImage scales to 1.03 over 280 ms behind a fixed frame, so the crop moves but the card does not. A gold hairline draws in along the bottom edge from left to right. Label shifts from off-white to gold.
Focus-visible2 px gold outline at 2 px offset around the whole card. Always present, never suppressed.
ActiveScale returns to 1.0 for 80 ms — a press-in, not a bounce.
TouchNo hover state at all. The hairline is permanently visible at 40% opacity so the card still reads as interactive.
LoadingSkeleton at the exact card dimensions, shimmering once every 1.6 s. The grid never reflows when images arrive.

No card lifts on hover, no card gains a shadow. Elevation is reserved for drawers and modals; if a card floats, the page loses its flatness and starts to look like a template.

6.2 Weight and purity display

FieldFormatRules
Weight8.4 gOne decimal place always, so 8.0 g and 8.4 g align. The unit is lowercase with a thin space. Tola or carat shown instead where the owner has set that unit on the piece.
Purity22K · 925Gold as 24K / 22K / 18K / 14K; silver as 925. No "carat" spelled out on a card — it collides with gemstone carat in the customer's head.
HallmarkSmall gold markShown on the detail page beside purity, not on the card. One mark per card is the limit.
Missing valuesOmittedA missing weight removes the segment and its separator. Never "—", never "N/A", never an empty gap.
Owner overrideGlobalWhether weight shows publicly at all is one setting, applied everywhere at once. This is an open decision in the architecture's Appendix C.

8.5 Performance rules for reels

Rules

  • Exit is 0.8× the enter duration. Things should leave faster than they arrive.
  • Animate opacity and transform only. Animating width, height or top causes layout work on every frame and janks on a mid-range phone.
  • Motion must mean something: it shows where a panel came from or confirms an action landed. Decorative motion is cut.
  • Scroll-triggered reveals: one fade-and-rise of 16 px, once, on section entry. No staggered cascades, no parallax, no counters ticking up.
  • prefers-reduced-motion collapses everything to a 100 ms opacity change and disables autoplay, hover previews and scroll reveals entirely. This is a requirement, not an enhancement.

14.3 Rules that hold at every width

  • No horizontal scroll, ever, except inside an element explicitly designed as a rail. A page that scrolls sideways at 320 px is a defect, not a compromise.
  • Pinch zoom is never disabled. Customers zoom into jewellery photographs; taking that away is hostile.
  • Images always declare their aspect ratio, so the layout never shifts as they arrive.
  • Tables and long strings — addresses, product codes — wrap rather than overflow.
  • Text scales by breakpoint step, not by viewport width. Fluid type makes headings unpredictable at odd widths.
  • Orientation change is handled: a phone turned landscape gets the tablet grid, and the reel viewer stays 9:16 letterboxed rather than stretching.
  • Safe areas are respected on notched devices — the floating WhatsApp sits above the home indicator, not under it.

16.2 Why the design survives a theme change

MechanismEffect
Tokens, not valuesColour, type, spacing, radius and motion live as tokens. A new theme adopts the token sheet and the look travels with it.
Components, not pagesThe product card, category card, reel tile and branch card are defined once and rendered by the plugin. A theme change re-skins their surroundings, not them.
Data pages are template-drivenProduct, category, reel, collection and showroom pages come from templates the plugin provides and a theme may override. Switching themes changes nothing on them unless the new theme chooses to override.

Switch-safety checklistThe eight-point check in the architecture runs before and after every theme change.
