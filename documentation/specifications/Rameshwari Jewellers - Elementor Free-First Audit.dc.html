Rameshwari Jewellers · Final OPEN-1 reassessment

Elementor Free-First Audit

Verified against official documentation. The 17-vs-21 question turns out to be the wrong measure — and the reason is significant.
The finding that matters most

The architecture already assumed a Free-only baseline, and that assumption holds — but one document does not. The Development Blueprint §12.1 lists eleven dynamic tags as a deliverable, and the Technical Blueprint §5.2 makes dynamic tags the primary mechanism for exposing product fields to builders. Dynamic tags are a Pro feature. Elementor's own help documentation gives them the URL elementor.com/help/dynamic-tags-pro/ — the suffix Elementor uses to mark Pro-only articles.

Nothing breaks: the locked architecture already requires that shortcodes are the compatibility floor and every widget has a shortcode and block equivalent (Development Blueprint §5.1, §12.3). So the fix is a documentation correction, not a redesign — the dynamic-tag layer is reclassified from base deliverable to optional Pro enhancement. Detail in §7.

1 · Official Free vs Pro matrix

Method, stated so you can check it

Elementor's knowledge base marks Pro-only articles with a -pro URL suffix — login-widget-pro, dynamic-tags-pro, custom-code-pro, nav-menu-widget-pro — while Free widgets carry none: heading-widget, shortcode-widget, html-widget. I read the full article index at elementor.com/help/elementor-pro-vs-free/ and classified from those slugs. The convention is strong but not absolute — a few known-Pro features (Loop Grid, Form, Popups, Taxonomy Filter, Custom CSS) carry no suffix, so those are marked from corroborating sources or left UNCLEAR. Where a capability is UNCLEAR, the architecture does not depend on it, which makes the uncertainty harmless rather than a risk to carry.

7 · Final OPEN-1 status

C — OPEN-1 should be replaced by a Free-first capability/integration model.

The evidence, not a preference. "17 or 21" measured how many widgets to build, which presumed the only question was scope. The audit shows the binding constraint is different: which capabilities exist in Elementor Free at all. Once that is settled, the widget count falls out as a consequence rather than a decision:

Old framingWhat the capability evidence shows
21 widgetsNot viable as stated. Four are duplicates or non-widgets, and Reel Viewer needs a popup mechanism Free does not have.
17 widgetsNearly right, two corrections. Mega Menu cannot be a widget — Free cannot build headers. Category Slider and Reel Slider are display modes, not data sources.
13 widgetsToo few. Omits Hero, Wishlist, Account and Notification Signup, each of which needs a capability Free lacks entirely (display conditions, Login, Form).
The capability model15 Free widgets · 15 shortcodes · 15 blocks · 3 theme components · 0 base dynamic tags. Derived from what Free provides, not chosen from a list.

10 · Final architecture statement

FINAL ELEMENTOR ARCHITECTURE: Elementor Free/Core is the required baseline and Elementor Pro is an optional future enhancement that may never become a dependency; the rameshwari-core plugin owns all business data and all business logic — products, the 306-term category hierarchy, the Model B resolver, search synonyms, reels, collections, showrooms and their hours, testimonials, scheduled hero campaigns, contact details, the WhatsApp engine, wishlist, customers, notification preferences, weight visibility, import and export, the activity log and health checks — and exposes them through shortcodes, Gutenberg blocks, fifteen Elementor-Free-compatible widgets and its own theme templates; where Elementor Free lacks a capability the platform needs — a theme builder for header and footer, dynamic field binding, loop queries, forms, login, popups — RJ supplies it in the plugin or the theme rather than requiring Pro, and the owner manages every business operation from the WordPress and RJ admin screens without a developer; Elementor is therefore a presentation and editing layer over RJ data, never the system of record, so business continuity survives Elementor Free being disabled or removed, Pro never being purchased, Pro being installed or removed later, or Elementor being replaced entirely — with page arrangement the only thing lost, rebuildable from the theme's block patterns, and pixel-identical design after Elementor removal explicitly not promised.
